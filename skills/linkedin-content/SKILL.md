# LinkedIn Content

Write LinkedIn posts that get reach in 2026. The core shift: **the algorithm now rewards evidence over emotion.** Motivational fluff and empty "broetry" (one sentence per line, no substance) are actively down-ranked. What wins is *operational depth, a contrarian point of view, and specific proof.*

## How reach actually works

LinkedIn tests every post on a small slice of your network in the **first ~90 minutes**, then expands or kills it based on early **quality** signals:
- **Dwell time** — did people stop and *read*, not scroll past. The hook buys this.
- **Substantive comments and saves** — a long comment or a save counts for far more than a like or a 👍.

The whole game: a hook that makes people stop, a body worth reading, an ending that makes people comment. Likes are noise.

## Anatomy of a post that works

1. **Hook (first 1–2 lines) — 80% of the outcome.** LinkedIn truncates at "…see more," so those lines decide reach. Make the first line a *specific, contrarian claim* — a surprising number, or a take people want to argue with or co-sign. Short (≤ ~50 characters), aimed at your audience's real pain. No throat-clearing, no context first.
2. **One idea per post.** Don't cram three insights. Pick one, go deep.
3. **Operational depth — replace emotion with evidence.** Back the claim with a *specific* story, decision, or number someone could learn from. "We cut onboarding from 6 days to 40 minutes by X" beats "we obsess over our customers." Real names, real figures, real moments.
4. **Format for skimming, not for spacing.** Short paragraphs and some line breaks help; *empty* one-thought-per-line spacing with no substance is now penalized. Every line earns its place.
5. **Close on a provocation.** End with a genuine question or a pointed claim that invites disagreement or a co-sign. This is the comment engine, and comments are the reach engine. Then **reply to every comment in the first hour** — early velocity compounds.
6. **Link in the first comment, never the body.** External links in the post body suppress reach (LinkedIn wants people to stay on-platform). Put the URL in your first comment and point to it from the post ("full piece in comments 👇"). Bonus: you can post before the destination is even live.

## Pick the format to match the goal

- **Story** → builds trust (a real sales call, hire, or failure).
- **Teardown** → shows specificity (walk through one decision in detail).
- **Framework / list** → earns saves (a repeatable how-to).
- **Opinion / contrarian take** → wins positioning (a defensible hot take).

Every strong post is also one **proof type**: problem proof, market proof, execution proof, or judgment proof. Know which one you're offering.

## The contrarian lever — use it, don't abuse it

Reach comes from debate, so a contrarian thesis is the strongest hook — but only when it's **defensible and backed by your own evidence.** "Raw coding skill is now overrated" works *if* every line under it is a real number. Empty ragebait gets flagged and burns trust. Contrarian + evidence = thought leadership. Contrarian + nothing = a troll.

## Do / Don't

**Do:** lead with the sharpest line; land one real number in the first three lines; write like a person, not a brand channel; post ~2×/week (one story, one teardown/opinion); reply fast.

**Don't:** bury the hook under context; stack multiple ideas; use generic inspiration; pad length with empty spacing; put the link in the body; use paid promotion to rescue a weak organic post.

## Voice is a separate job

This skill is about *structure and strategy*, not voice. If the post goes out under a specific person's name, pair it with their voice guide (e.g., a `writing-like-<name>` skill) to nail the voice first, then apply this skill's structure. Keep the two jobs separate — don't let one blur the other.

## Worked hooks (before → after)

- ❌ "Excited to share some thoughts on how AI is changing engineering!"
  ✅ "We've written 100% of our production code with AI this year. It's making one kind of engineer obsolete."
- ❌ "Customer obsession is everything to us."
  ✅ "We flew to our customer's warehouse and watched them click through hell at 4am. Then we shipped the fix."

Specific, contrarian, and each promises a payoff. That's the whole hook.
